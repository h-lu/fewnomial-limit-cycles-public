# Reproducing the limit-cycle bounds

The complete local edition is `fewnomial-r37.zip`. It contains the four PDFs, their editable inputs, the scientific replay ZIP and `SHA256SUMS`. The smaller `fewnomial-replay.zip` contains the same scientific inputs and reference outputs with its own source manifest. Unpack either archive in a fresh directory, then run

~~~sh
sha256sum --strict -c SHA256SUMS
make all
make freeze
~~~

The article is `main.tex`, with the degree-capped supplement in `low-N-supplement.tex`. Two independent attachments, `unrestricted-fields.tex` and `binary-saddle-tree.tex`, retain the unrestricted degree records and the single-row saddle-tree study respectively. Their internal numbering uses U and B. The four documents compile separately. The article and its degree-capped supplement contain the inputs for the main folded result and its finite comparisons.

`make verify` evaluates the finite allocations, small fields, return inequalities and independently decodes the fixed fields. `make comparisons` checks the support calculations and arithmetic of the comparison formulas. `make all` runs both, typesets the four PDFs and validates their labels, links and printed tables. `make freeze` constructs both ZIP files and verifies the release manifest. The outer ZIP is identified by its own file digest. All checks run offline with the dependencies below. The additional command `python3 -B -m research.mechanical_transfer.verify` checks the archived transfer algebra.

The reference software versions are CPython 3.14.4, python-flint 0.9.0 with FLINT 3.6.0, SymPy 1.14.0 with mpmath 1.3.0, and pypdf 6.18.0 for the document tests. The pinned packages' combined declared Python floor is 3.10. The tests here used the reference interpreter. Use a compatible python-flint binary wheel for the interpreter and architecture. Building that extension from source requires a C build toolchain and FLINT development libraries with their GMP and MPFR dependencies. See the project's installation instructions for its native dependency versions. [python-flint 0.9.0 release and platform wheels](https://pypi.org/project/python-flint/0.9.0/)

For offline installation, put the compatible wheels for the pinned packages and their dependencies in a local directory. With that directory mounted at `/path/to/wheels` run the following in a virtual environment. The PDFs additionally use GNU make, pdfLaTeX, BibTeX and Poppler. The shared preamble uses Latin Modern, microtype, mathtools, booktabs, enumitem and xcolor alongside the standard LaTeX packages. The main article also uses PGF/TikZ. Both diagrams are drawn from editable TeX. Each PDF is independently typeset. Printed cross-document references are validated against the destination labels. The article, supplement and binary note also link to named destinations in sibling PDFs. Place the PDFs side by side with their original names to follow those links.

~~~sh
python3 -m pip install --no-index --find-links=/path/to/wheels -r requirements.txt
make verify
~~~

In a hexadecimal field file, the lines `P` and `Q` begin the two components. Each data row gives the two nonnegative exponents and the signed numerator and positive denominator of its coefficient, all in base 16. The degree files contain one hexadecimal integer and a newline. The signed bounds in the 783 bracket table use denominator \(2^{20}\). If the two mantissas are \(a,b\) and the shift is \(s\), the \(w\)-interval is \([a2^s,b2^s]\) and the local energy is \(h=\pm w^{58}\). In the auxiliary quadrature table the last column is an upper bound on the absolute relative error in units of \(2^{-36}\). All signs used by the programs are strict bounds in rational or ball arithmetic.

To reproduce the complete integer allocation at 512 terms, run `python3 finite.py --stages 512`. Proposition 6.3 uses separate strips. The literature-field check expands the odd force for one rational shift sequence at depths one through five and verifies both term/degree allocations for integer caps through 2499. The general coefficients follow from the quantified proof. Lemma U5.3 and Table U3 identify the seventeen fixed brackets by their exact hexadecimal numerators. The PDF check reads the numerators back and compares them with the interval input.

## Primary sources and finite arithmetic

- Gasull and Santana's Nonlinearity paper concerns exactly counted component monomials at unrestricted degree. Theorem 1 gives the quadratic bound. The stronger finite floor in the article is the integer maximum within Proposition 1. The theorem statements and construction can be read in the [institutional postprint](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/artpub/2024/307723/GasSan2024-postprint.pdf) and [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.04281v3). The preceding monomial result is [Buzzi, Carvalho and Gasull, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07692). For the doubling idea see [Han and Romanovski, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.6470).
- The [Xiong–Han publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022039614002733) establishes the topic of their degree result. The [Chang–Zhang–Han publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022247X1300601X) specifies odd multiplicity. For [Álvarez, Coll, De Maesschalck and Prohens](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/30058?mode=simple) the repository describes the generalized Liénard canard construction and restricts access to the published PDF. The proof pages for these three articles were unavailable. No coefficient or finite numerical entry in the manuscripts uses their seeds.
- The two-source author TeX at the [frozen public commit](https://github.com/h-lu/hilbert16-two-source-renewal/blob/4b85f6184ccac8d073de2e1c3c74bd36308f5e58/manuscripts/two_source_lower_bound/manuscript.tex) matches the [3 October Zenodo edition](primary/zenodo-23113313/README.md). Theorem A, the transplant and the fourfold field are the inputs for S1.1. In [Qin and Sun v1, 8 October](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12218v1), Theorem 1.1 and Sections 3.2, 6.2–6.4 provide the degree count, polynomial perturbation and hyperbolic realization. The mechanical main PDF in the [November edition](primary/zenodo-24195238/README.md) supplies Proposition 3.3. S1.2 counts sufficient term ceilings for the displayed Qin–Sun and mechanical perturbations.
- The finite classical seed is a preprint. Theorem 1.1 gives at least \(\lceil(7n-48)/2\rceil\) hyperbolic cycles on an open set at exact primitive degree \(n\ge14\), with linear restoring force. The text is dated 24 November. S1.4 proves the sparse replication and gives its exact filling and degree. The same section gives a published finite seed example using [Huzak and De Maesschalck, Theorem 1.1, p. 2](https://real.mtak.hu/22977/1/p3307.pdf). [Classical preprint author source, v1.0.0](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/v1.0.0/source/main.tex)
- At all 65,489 integers from 48 through 65,536, the folded allocations agree with independent exhaustive-prefix maxima. The last-eight-stage estimate in Section 4.2 applies from 32,768 onwards. The first comparisons with the separate-strip and classical preprint formulas in [the dyadic grid](outputs/dyadic-comparisons.tsv) are defined on powers of two from 64 to 1,048,576. The JSON field `uniform` only selects among the elementary, stationary and folded formulas. The analytic constant 50 follows from Section 5.
